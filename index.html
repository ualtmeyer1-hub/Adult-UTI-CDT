    Evidence Grading Frameworks

    1. UKHSA Primary Care Tools (Oxford OCEBM Grades)

    • Grade A (OCEBM Level 1a/1b): Consistent Level 1 evidence; high diagnostic accuracy (e.g. 'DO NOT DIPSTICK' mandates in ≥65y & CAUTI).
    • Grade B (OCEBM Level 2a/2b/3): Consistent cohort/case-control studies (e.g. UKHSA/NICE 'Rule of Three' — dysuria, nocturia, cloudy urine; male LUTS; pyelonephritis).
    • Grade C / D / E: Case series (C), physiological first principles (D), or multidisciplinary expert consensus (E, e.g. hypothermia ≤36.0°C in elderly).
    • Grade Nat / N: Covered under existing national guidance (NICE NG109 / NG110 / NG111 / NG113 / QS90).

    2. SIGN 160 Evidence Levels & Recommendation Strengths

    • Level 1++ / 1+ [Strong Recommendation R]: High-quality meta-analyses, systematic reviews of RCTs, or RCTs with very low/low risk of bias ('should' be performed — e.g. 3-day treatment duration, non-treatment of ASB in non-pregnant women).
    • Level 2++ / 2+ [Strong / Conditional R]: High-quality systematic reviews or cohort/case-control studies supporting diagnostic likelihood ratios.
    • Level 3 / 4 & Good Practice Point [✓]: Non-analytic studies, expert consensus, or recommended best practice.

    3. UK SMI B 41 (Issue 8.8, Dec 2025)

    • National Accredited Laboratory Standard: Sets cohort-stratified colony thresholds (≥102 cfu/mL nephrostomy; ≥103 cfu/mL males & urostomy; ≥104 cfu/mL symptomatic females; ≥105 cfu/mL CAUTI & pregnancy) and boric acid 10 mL fill-line rules.
