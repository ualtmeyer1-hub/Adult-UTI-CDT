Understanding Your Urine Test: Getting the Right Care for Urine Infections (UTIs)

Why we sometimes recommend hydration and careful checks instead of a urine dipstick, urine culture, or antibiotics.

When you or a loved one feels unwell, it is natural to want quick answers and the right treatment. This guide explains how healthcare teams decide whether a urine test (called a urine culture) is needed for a suspected “waterworks” infection (Urinary Tract Infection, or UTI), and why making the right test choice keeps you safe.

What is a Urine Infection (UTI)?

A UTI happens when germs that normally live harmlessly in the gut enter the bladder or kidneys and cause active illness. Common signs of a UTI include:

  • Pain or burning when passing urine (peeing)
  • Needing to pee very often, passing only a small amount each time
  • A sudden, strong urge to pee that cannot be put off
  • New or worsening leaking (incontinence)
  • Pain in the lower tummy or lower back / side

Sometimes a UTI causes a high temperature (fever) and shivering. When this happens, confusion can be an additional symptom alongside these signs.

What is a Urine Culture?

A urine culture is a laboratory test that looks for the specific germs causing an infection. If germs grow in the lab, they are tested against different antibiotics so your clinician knows which medicine will work best.

Why Don’t We Always Do a Urine Dipstick or Culture?

It can feel surprising when a nurse or doctor decides not to test urine right away, especially if you have been told in the past that confusion or a fall always means a urine infection. Here is why practice has changed:

1. “Germs Without Symptoms” (Asymptomatic Bacteriuria)

Up to half of older adults and almost everyone with a long-term urinary catheter have harmless germs living in their bladder without an active infection. Treating these harmless germs with antibiotics does not make people feel better—it only causes side effects and makes future infections harder to treat.

2. Misleading Signs (Confusion, Falls, or Smelly/Dark Urine)
  • Dipstick strips frequently turn “positive” in older adults even when there is no infection.
  • Dark, cloudy, or strong-smelling urine on its own is usually a sign of not drinking enough fluids, not an infection.
  • New confusion, agitation, or a recent fall on their own are very common in older adults and are usually triggered by dehydration, constipation, pain, medication changes, or another illness—not a UTI—unless accompanied by bladder symptoms or a fever.
3. Avoiding Unnecessary Antibiotic Harm

Taking antibiotics when there is no bacterial infection can cause nausea, thrush, rashes, or serious gut infections (such as C. diff), and encourages antibiotic-resistant “superbugs”.

When IS a Urine Culture Test Really Important?

Healthcare teams prioritise urine cultures when the result will genuinely guide safe treatment—for example, when there are:

  • New pain when peeing, or two or more new bladder symptoms (such as urgency, frequency, new leaking, or lower tummy pain)
  • Serious signs of infection, such as a high temperature, shivering/rigors, flank pain, or signs of sepsis
  • Specific health risks, such as pregnancy, a kidney transplant, or a urinary catheter alongside new signs of being unwell
  • Symptoms that do not improve after initial treatment

How You Can Help Us Get the Best Sample

If your clinician advises that a urine culture is needed, collecting a clean sample avoids false results:

  • Mid-stream sample: Pass a small amount of urine into the toilet first, then collect the middle portion into the sample pot (ask staff for the step-by-step leaflet if needed).
  • Catheter samples: Staff will take the sample directly from the catheter sampling port—never from the drainage bag—and will change the catheter first if it has been in place for more than 7 days.
  • Red-top sample pot: Filled up to the marked line so the preservative works properly.
  • Tell us your symptoms: Let your doctor or nurse know exactly what new symptoms you or your loved one are experiencing.

Our Commitment to Your Safety

By only sending urine tests and prescribing antibiotics when they are truly needed, your healthcare team is protecting you from unnecessary medication side effects and ensuring antibiotics continue to work when you really need them. If you have any questions, please ask your doctor or nurse.
